# 4. USER REGISTRATION AND AUTHENTICATION

Implement a secure OTP-based authentication system.

## 4.1 OTP login

Normal users must log in using OTP rather than a conventional password.

Support:

- Email OTP.
- Mobile SMS OTP.
- A choice of available verification channel on the login screen.
- OTP resend functionality.
- OTP expiration.
- OTP attempt limits.
- Rate limiting.
- Protection against OTP brute-force attacks.
- Secure OTP verification.
- Clear success and failure messages.

OTP delivery must use configurable server-side email and SMS providers.

Do not hardcode API keys or provider credentials.

If a delivery provider has not been configured, show a clear setup error rather than pretending that OTP was sent.

## 4.2 Single-device login enforcement

One user account must have only one active authenticated device/session at a time.

Example:

1. User logs in on Laptop A.
2. Laptop A receives an authenticated session.
3. The same user logs in on Mobile B.
4. The previous Laptop A session is immediately revoked.
5. Laptop A must no longer be able to access protected pages or APIs.
6. Mobile B becomes the only active session.

Requirements:

- Maintain the active session on the backend.
- Revoke the previous session when a new login succeeds.
- Validate the session on every protected backend request.
- Do not rely only on frontend state or localStorage.
- Show a suitable message when an account is logged in elsewhere.
- Handle concurrent login requests safely.
- Provide an admin option to revoke a user's active session.

## 4.3 Automatic logout after 30 days

Every authenticated user session must have a strict maximum lifetime of 30 days.

- Enforce expiry on the backend.
- Do not extend the 30-day maximum merely because the user is active.
- After expiry, require fresh OTP verification.
- Clear the client session securely.
- Prevent expired sessions from accessing protected APIs.
- Display an appropriate session-expired message.

## 4.4 Account security

- Secure session cookies where supported.
- Use HTTPS in production.
- Protect against CSRF, XSS, and common authentication attacks.
- Never expose OTP values, session tokens, or private credentials in logs.
- Implement logout functionality.
- Implement session revocation.
- Protect against account enumeration where practical.

# 9. NUMEROLOGY CALCULATION METHODOLOGY

IMPORTANT: Follow these rules exactly.

Do not substitute a different numerology system.

Do not introduce arbitrary calculations from external numerology conventions.

All formulas must be centralized in the numerology engine and covered by tests.

## 9.1 Basic Number / Mulank

Calculate by adding the digits of the day of birth until a single digit remains.

Example:

Date of birth: 13/08/1994

1 + 3 = 4

Basic Number = 4

The calculation uses the birth day only.

## 9.2 Destiny Number / Bhagyank

Add every digit of the complete date of birth until a single digit remains.

Example:

13/08/1994

1 + 3 + 0 + 8 + 1 + 9 + 9 + 4 = 35

3 + 5 = 8

Destiny Number = 8

## 9.3 Natal Grid

Use this fixed grid layout:

```text
3 | 1 | 9
---------
6 | 7 | 5
---------
2 | 8 | 4
```

### Natal grid calculation rules

1. Take the complete date of birth.
2. For the Natal Grid only, exclude the century digits from the birth year.
3. Include the remaining date digits.
4. Ignore zero.
5. Add the Basic Number.
6. Add the Destiny Number.
7. Place each number in its fixed position.
8. Preserve repeated occurrences.
9. Leave absent numbers blank.
10. Do not remove duplicates.

Example:

DOB = 13/08/1994

Exclude century digits "19" from the birth year.

Remaining DOB digits:

1, 3, 0, 8, 9, 4

Ignore zero.

Add Basic Number = 4.

Add Destiny Number = 8.

Final digits:

1, 3, 8, 9, 4, 4, 8

Natal Grid:

```text
3 | 1 | 9
---------
  |   |
---------
  | 88| 44
```

The Natal Grid is permanent and does not change with years or dates.

# 10. MAHADASHA CALCULATION

The first Mahadasha begins with the Basic Number.

Its duration in years equals the number itself.

After that, follow the normal number sequence:

4 → 5 → 6 → 7 → 8 → 9 → 1 → 2 → 3

The sequence must wrap around after 9.

Continue until the individual reaches age 45.

At age 45, restart the sequence from the original Basic Number.

### Example

DOB = 13/08/1994

Basic Number = 4.

| Mahadasha | Start Date | End Date |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 13 Aug 1994 | 12 Aug 1998 |
| 5 | 13 Aug 1998 | 12 Aug 2003 |
| 6 | 13 Aug 2003 | 12 Aug 2009 |
| 7 | 13 Aug 2009 | 12 Aug 2016 |
| 8 | 13 Aug 2016 | 12 Aug 2024 |
| 9 | 13 Aug 2024 | 12 Aug 2033 |
| 1 | 13 Aug 2033 | 12 Aug 2034 |
| 2 | 13 Aug 2034 | 12 Aug 2036 |
| 3 | 13 Aug 2036 | 12 Aug 2039 |

At age 45:

13 Aug 2039 → Restart from 4.

The active Mahadasha number must be included in the relevant annual, monthly, and daily grids.

# 11. PERSONAL YEAR / ANTARDASHA

The Personal Year changes on every birthday.

It remains constant from one birthday until the day before the next birthday.

### Formula

Add:

1. All digits of the birth day.
2. All digits of the birth month.
3. Individual digits of the last two digits of the current calendar year.
4. The weekday number of the birthday in that calendar year.

Reduce the total to a single digit.

### Weekday number mapping

| Weekday | Number |
|---|---:|
| Sunday | 1 |
| Monday | 2 |
| Tuesday | 9 |
| Wednesday | 5 |
| Thursday | 3 |
| Friday | 6 |
| Saturday | 8 |

Numbers 4 and 7 are not used in this weekday mapping.

### Example

DOB = 13/08/1994

Calculate the Personal Year for 13 Aug 2026 to 12 Aug 2027.

13 Aug 2026 is Thursday.

1 + 3 + 8 + 2 + 6 + 3 = 23

2 + 3 = 5

Personal Year = 5.

The Personal Year remains 5 from:

13 Aug 2026 to 12 Aug 2027.

# 13. PRATYANTAR DASHA / MONTHLY DASHA

IMPORTANT: This is not a normal calendar-month calculation.

It represents periods of 8 to 72 days.

### Calculation rules

1. Start from the current Personal Year number.
2. Increase the number sequentially.
3. After 9, return to 1.
4. Duration of each period = current sequence number × 8 days.
5. Continue until the total reaches 360 days.
6. The remaining 5 days of a standard 365-day year remain blank.
7. For a leap-year interval, the remaining days may be 6.

### Example

Personal Year = 5.

Sequence:

| Number | Duration |
|---|---:|
| 5 | 40 days |
| 6 | 48 days |
| 7 | 56 days |
| 8 | 64 days |
| 9 | 72 days |
| 1 | 8 days |
| 2 | 16 days |
| 3 | 24 days |
| 4 | 32 days |

Total = 360 days.

### Example dates

Annual period:

13 Aug 2026 to 12 Aug 2027.

| Pratyantar | Start Date | End Date |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | 13 Aug 2026 | 21 Sep 2026 |
| 6 | 22 Sep 2026 | 08 Nov 2026 |
| 7 | 09 Nov 2026 | 03 Jan 2027 |
| 8 | 04 Jan 2027 | 08 Mar 2027 |
| 9 | 09 Mar 2027 | 19 May 2027 |
| 1 | 20 May 2027 | 27 May 2027 |
| 2 | 28 May 2027 | 12 Jun 2027 |
| 3 | 13 Jun 2027 | 06 Jul 2027 |
| 4 | 07 Jul 2027 | 07 Aug 2027 |
| Blank | 08 Aug 2027 | 12 Aug 2027 |

Each Pratyantar period must have its own grid.

The Pratyantar Grid includes:

- Natal.
- Mahadasha.
- Personal Year.
- Active Pratyantar.

All repeated occurrences must be preserved.

Use correct date arithmetic and test leap-year scenarios.

# 14. DAY LORD CALCULATION

Day Lord changes every calendar day.

### Formula

Day Lord = Active Pratyantar Number + Weekday Number.

Use the same weekday mapping defined in the Personal Year section.

Example:

13 Aug 2026:

- Active Pratyantar = 5.
- Thursday = 3.

Day Lord = 5 + 3 = 8.

The Day Lord must be recalculated for each calendar day.

IMPORTANT: The exact reduction rule for sums greater than 9 has not yet been specified.

Do not silently assume whether such a sum should be reduced to one digit or retained as its raw sum.

Implement this rule as a clearly isolated, configurable calculation policy. Mark the unresolved behavior in documentation and tests until the methodology owner confirms it.
